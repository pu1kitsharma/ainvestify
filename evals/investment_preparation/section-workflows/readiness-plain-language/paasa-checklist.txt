Paasa — records to request and checks to run

These are proposed checks, not completed findings.

1. What money does Paasa actually keep?
Service: Apex Plan
Check whether the income from this service covers the direct cost of providing it.

Ask the founders for
- Customer bills, refunds and payment records for the last six completed months, with personal details removed.
- Supplier and partner bills, plus staff and support costs for the same work.
- Agreements showing which company earns the fees and what must be paid to others.

What to do with the records
1. Work out the income the company actually earns. Separate money held for customers, taxes passed on, and money owed to partners.
2. Subtract the direct costs of providing the service. Count each cost once; compare the same service, month and currency.

What this helps decide
Make a monthly table of income, direct costs and the amount left. If costs use up the income, investigate pricing and delivery costs before recommending expansion.

Leave missing amounts unanswered. This check excludes fixed head-office costs, so the amount left is not the company’s overall profit.

2. Do new customers enroll in the service?
Service: Apex Plan
Find out how often a new sign-up turns into the customer action being checked.

Ask the founders for
- A customer list for the last six completed months, with sign-up dates and service plans. Use anonymous customer IDs.
- Service-enrollment records with the same customer IDs, dates and active or cancelled status.

What to do with the records
1. Agree which customers belong in the comparison and how service enrollment differs from initial account sign-up.
2. Use only customers with a full 90 days of records after sign-up. Count each customer once if they enrolled in the service within those 90 days.
3. Divide that count by all qualifying new customers with a full 90 days of records. Show the percentage by sign-up month and service plan.

What this helps decide
Use the result to choose where to investigate customers getting stuck. Agree an improvement target with the founders using the actual results.

Missing records mean the answer is unknown. This check does not establish repeat use, customer satisfaction or why customers stopped.
