## Research memo: pursue a focused founder conversation

Paasa describes a platform for international investing, combining trading access with an advisory offering. Its company profile identifies affluent Indian customers facing remittance, guidance and tax-filing friction. Those are reported customer problems; the supplied evidence does not establish how many customers experience them or whether Paasa has solved them. [Company profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/paasa)

The published plans imply two commercial questions. Access customers incur transaction charges without a recurring plan fee. Apex has an asset-based annual advisory charge collected monthly; brokerage remains separate, with no brokerage markup for Apex customers. This is a pricing description, not evidence of realized company revenue. [Pricing](https://paasa.com/pricing)

The site identifies Arc Spire Advisory as the advisory service entity and Interactive Brokers as a service provider. The investment target's legal entity, ownership of revenue and partner economics still need to be established from company records. A brand, its operating entities and its partners should not be treated as one financial statement. [Company disclosures](https://paasa.com/)

**Why spend time on it?** A repeatable business is plausible if transaction activity or sustained advisory relationships produce retained income exceeding the variable cost of serving those customers. The question is which customer segment and service can do this consistently. An Access customer does not need to upgrade for the business to earn transaction-related income; the amount retained and frequency of activity remain unknown.

**What could weaken the case?** Infrequent trading could limit Access income while customers still require support. Advisory customers could withdraw assets or require costly service. These are hypotheses to test, not observed failures. Dependence on partner terms makes the revenue reconciliation important; a partner relationship alone is not evidence of a problem.

**Current decision:** request a short discovery call and the two evidence packages below before recommending a fundraising engagement. The packet contains no usable time series for company revenue, funded customers, retention or delivery costs. Rapid growth, contribution profit, valuation and a suitable raise amount therefore remain unassessed. A missing public metric is not a zero.

## Diligence and readiness work

### 1. Establish retained income and the cost to deliver it

**Founder question:** what income does the proposed investment entity retain from each plan, and what variable costs are necessary to earn it?

**Request:** for the latest six completed months, provide redacted fee invoices or billing exports, credit notes, collection and partner-settlement statements, and the corresponding revenue ledger. Include month, currency, plan, anonymized account key, fee type, refunds and tax components. Provide the relevant fee-sharing agreements and an entity chart showing who contracts, invoices and receives income. Add direct service-cost records and a documented allocation for staff who serve these accounts. Reuse the same period and account keys across the files. Partial availability should be identified, not filled with estimates presented as facts.

**Work and deliverable:** reconcile billings to collections, refunds and unsettled balances; map income to the relevant entity and document gross/net revenue treatment. Prepare a monthly bridge by plan from recognized revenue to contribution profit after attributable variable costs. Exclude customer principal and assets from business revenue; avoid deducting partner charges twice when the starting revenue figure is already net. Separate one-off onboarding from ongoing service costs and disclose allocation assumptions. This approach follows the need to make fintech revenue presentation and variable-cost treatment explicit. [Methodology: Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/fintech-entrepreneurs-guide-to-creating-enterprise-value-starts-with-contribution-profit)

**Decision:** identify a service/customer segment with a credible repeatable economic model, or prioritize pricing, partner terms or service-cost changes before fundraising. Negative contribution in the observed period requires investigation; it is not by itself proof that future scale is impossible. No financial result is calculated until records exist.

### 2. Test whether customers fund and continue using the service

**Founder question:** which acquisition cohorts become funded customers and continue the behavior relevant to their plan?

**Request:** for the same period, provide a redacted account-event export with registration date, first funding date, plan changes, trading activity, advisory enrollment/cancellation, deposits and withdrawals, plus any existing acquisition-channel tags and customer exit reasons. Use anonymized account keys. Do not send identity documents. Mark missing fields and distinguish inactive accounts from closed ones.

**Work and deliverable:** produce registration-to-funding conversion by monthly cohort; repeat trading for Access; and continued advisory enrollment for Apex. Compare cohorts only over equal observation windows, excluding those not yet old enough for each window. Explain asset changes using flows and market movement where records support that separation. Provide a cohort table and a ranked list of measurable funnel gaps. Logs describe behavior; documented customer feedback is needed to investigate why it happened.

**Decision:** choose a specific activation or retention experiment for the segment with demonstrated demand, or defer an acquisition push if most cohorts fail to fund or continue. Define a success threshold with the founders from observed baselines before running an experiment; do not invent a benchmark or claim the experiment has been executed.
