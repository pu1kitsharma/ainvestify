# Final-work audit rubric

Audit final generated prose against the captured source record, not the model's own pass verdict. Preserve problematic passages in the audit and record whether they affect a decision. A coding-agent audit is development evaluation, not independent investor certification.

- Business: describes the actual target offering/customer and distinguishes partner roles; attributes reported claims and promised benefits.
- Economics: identifies payer and charging mechanism, distinguishes gross flows and third-party charges from retained company revenue; identifies a precise remaining financial question.
- Investment argument: contains a conditional commercial reason to engage, a company-specific risk and a decision evidence could resolve adversely. A repeated product description fails.
- Founder opening: explains a relevant observation naturally and accurately. No fabricated relationship, verified impact or investor commitment.
- Founder proposal: offers a concrete deliverable that addresses this business and asks for a conversation. Generic landscape research without an operating decision fails.
- Diligence A/B: requests distinct, identifiable company-held records with scope and explains the decision they enable. Source IDs, metric labels and repeated questions fail.
- Readiness A/B: uses its linked records for specific analysis; names an output and a decision admitting adverse findings. Collecting the same records again is not the analysis.

Record factual errors, actionable section count, section completion, calls, correction attempts and elapsed time separately. Do not convert missing financial inputs to zero performance. Do not equate completed drafts with investment readiness or a closed financing. Select a model only after every required case is completed and audited under the same protocol/configuration.

Financial reasoning checks: published tariffs, billed revenue, cash collections, customer assets and company-retained revenue are different quantities. A proposed margin calculation must identify its revenue basis and comparable-period costs; do not subtract currency costs from tariff percentages or count partner charges twice. Distinguish gross profit from contribution profit when including additional variable service costs. An unknown partner split does not imply zero revenue or unprofitability. Bessemer's [fintech contribution-profit guide](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/fintech-entrepreneurs-guide-to-creating-enterprise-value-starts-with-contribution-profit) explains why the business model, revenue presentation and variable-cost allocation matter; it is methodology, not evidence about an evaluated company.

Decision checks: retention records measure behavior, not the cause of churn. Claims about currency changes, regulation, pricing or a product feature causing retention require additional evidence or an explicitly proposed investigation. An adverse operating result should change a specified decision, not automatically prove that a company is uninvestable. The [YC seed fundraising guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round) provides fundraising preparation context; generated materials alone do not establish traction, investor demand or a completed financing.
